**Product Hunt is one launch surface, not a launch strategy.** A strong day on Product Hunt can bring a spike of signups — but the traffic fades within 48 hours, the listing stops sending visitors once it leaves the front page, and nothing about it compounds. In 2026 the founders who grow steadily treat Product Hunt as one row in a stack: a permanent directory listing for compounding discovery, a community where their buyers actually live, and a launch platform for the spike.

This guide compares the seven alternatives worth your time, what each one is genuinely good at, and how to combine them without burning two weeks.

## Why founders look beyond Product Hunt

Three things changed the calculus:

1. **Launch day is a spike, not a channel.** Daily rankings reset. A product that finishes mid-pack on a busy Tuesday gets a few hundred visitors and then goes quiet.
2. **Buyers moved into AI assistants.** A growing share of "what's the best tool for X?" research happens in ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini — none of which read a 48-hour leaderboard ([GEO/AIEO guide](/blog/geo-aieo-get-recommended-by-ai)).
3. **Compounding beats spiking.** A permanent, indexed, dofollow listing keeps sending qualified traffic and keeps building authority months after launch week ([why dofollow directory links matter](/blog/dofollow-directory-backlinks-saas)).

None of that makes Product Hunt worthless. It makes it _one input_.

## 1. Curated launch directories

A directory listing is the closest thing to an annuity in launch distribution. You submit once, and the listing keeps working: it gets crawled, it ranks for your category, and it passes a permanent backlink.

This is what aat.ee is built around — a permanent dofollow listing, category placement, and optional GEO/AIEO tiers that put you in front of the assistants people now ask for recommendations. It is the lowest-effort, longest-lived item on this list, and the one that keeps paying in month six.

**Choose this when:** you want discovery that outlives launch week, and you care about being citable by AI assistants.

## 2. Product Hunt

Still the biggest single-day audience in tech, and the badge still carries social proof. The tradeoffs are real: rankings reset daily, competition for the top five is fierce, and the link is nofollow — so its SEO value is indirect.

**Choose this when:** you have a genuinely novel product, a following you can mobilize at 12:01am PT, and you want a spike plus the "featured on Product Hunt" line.

## 3. Founder communities

Indie Hackers, r/SideProject, relevant Discord and Slack groups, and niche forums. You will not get a traffic spike. You will get something more valuable early on: real feedback and a handful of users who tell you why they did or didn't sign up.

**Choose this when:** you are pre-traction and need signal, not scale. Lead with the problem, not the link.

## 4. Industry-specific directories

A fintech directory, a design-tool gallery, a developer-tool index — these are smaller, but the visitor is already shopping your category. Conversion rates are often several times higher than a general launch site.

**Choose this when:** a credible niche directory exists for your category. Check its [domain rating](/blog/domain-rating-dr-ai-visibility) and whether links are dofollow before paying.

## 5. Newsletter features

A mention in a curated newsletter reaches an opted-in audience with no algorithmic gatekeeper. It is the warmest traffic on this list and often free — if your story is specific enough ("we replaced a $4,000/yr tool with a $9/mo one") rather than generic ("we launched our app").

**Choose this when:** you have one sharp, concrete story to tell. Pitch ten newsletters; expect two yeses.

## 6. Marketplaces and app stores

If your product fits a platform — a browser extension, a Slack app, a Shopify app, a Figma plugin — the marketplace is a distribution channel you can ride for years. The tradeoff is a revenue share and someone else's rules.

**Choose this when:** your product genuinely lives inside another platform, and you accept the platform risk.

## 7. Your own launch page, indexed and citable

The only surface you fully control. Publish a clean launch page with a one-sentence description, a comparison table, and an FAQ, then make it easy for crawlers to find ([what AI crawlers want](/blog/ai-crawlers-robots-llms-txt-guide)).

**Choose this when:** always. It is the foundation every other item on this list points back to.

## The stack, not the platform

A realistic 10-day sequence that avoids the "launch and vanish" trap:

- **Days 1–3** — Submit to curated directories (permanent listing, indexed, dofollow). Publish your own launch page.
- **Days 4–6** — Post in two or three founder communities. Ask for feedback, not upvotes.
- **Days 7–10** — Pitch newsletters with one specific story. Submit to any credible niche directory you found.
- **Then** — Schedule Product Hunt for the day you can genuinely mobilize an audience. Not before.

The order matters: permanent assets first, spike last. If launch day is quiet, the compounding work is already done.

## What actually matters when you choose

Ignore total visitor numbers; they are mostly vanity. Compare on these instead:

- **Is the link permanent and dofollow?** This is the whole ballgame for compounding authority.
- **Is the listing indexed and category-placed?** A listing in the right category ranks for the queries your buyers type.
- **Can an AI assistant find it?** If the page is not crawlable and extractable, it does not exist for assistant answers.
- **Who else is listed?** A directory full of your competitors is a feature — it is where your buyers are shopping.
- **What does it cost after month one?** Free listings that stay free beat paid placements that expire.

## FAQ

**Is Product Hunt still worth it in 2026?**
Yes — as one input. It can deliver a real spike and useful social proof, but a nofollow link and a daily-resetting ranking mean it will not compound. Do it alongside permanent listings, not instead of them.

**What is the single best Product Hunt alternative?**
For most SaaS and AI products, a permanent dofollow listing on a category-relevant directory. It is low-effort, keeps working for months, and makes you citable by both search engines and AI assistants.

**How many places should I launch on?**
Five or six, deliberately chosen and properly set up — one or two permanent directories, two or three communities, one spike platform. Fifteen half-finished submissions beat nothing; five good ones beat fifteen.
